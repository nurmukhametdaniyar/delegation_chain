## Summary for the author

By the frozen rule, BLS aggregation is **not a net benefit**. In the deployment pattern the plan's §1 asks about (arm B against arm D, warm+prefix, N = 3, medium), the latency ratio is {{vr:bd:3:medium}}, and every run agrees ({{vruns:bd:3:medium}}). It is not a net benefit in any cell, with caching or without. B is {{vxrange:bd}}× slower than D ({{vcount:bd:not a net benefit}} cells). Warm, A is {{vxrange:ac}}× slower than C and {{vxrange:acb}}× slower than C-batch ({{vcount:ac:not a net benefit}} and {{vcount:acb:not a net benefit}} cells), and cold it is {{vxrange:acc}}× slower than C. No N from 1 to 10 reverses this, although the gap narrows as N grows. With the pairing and prefix caches, B's hit still costs one pairing check, {{latrange:B:warm+prefix:medium}} µs whatever N is (medium), where D's hit costs one Ed25519 verification, {{latrange:D:warm+prefix:medium}} µs.

Aggregation saves bytes against Ed25519 only from {{breakeven:small}} in the small profile, {{breakeven:medium}} in medium and {{breakeven:large}} in large, and from {{breakeven:medium-approval}} when the chain carries an approval receipt. At N = 1, A's chain is larger ({{bdiff:1:medium}} bytes, medium), because every body carries 48-byte BLS keys where Ed25519's are 32. The saving stays small. At N = 3 (medium), A's chain is {{bytes:A:3:medium}} bytes against C's {{bytes:C:3:medium}}, {{bsave:3:medium}}% smaller, and {{bsave:10:medium}}% smaller at N = 10, because A's {{sig:A:3:medium}}-byte aggregate is only {{share:A:3:medium}}% of the chain. Against individual BLS signatures, aggregation saves {{ablhop:medium}} bytes per hop, {{ablshare:3:medium}}% of A-ind's chain at N = 3.

Of the paper claims checked against revision 2026-09-29, one is **not supported**: that cheap checks reject hostile chains before any pairing. It holds only on a warm verifier, because a cold one first pays its N + 1 certificate pairings (P-28). The others are supported: aggregation's saving over individual BLS (A/A-ind {{v3range:aai:n2plus}} warm, N ≥ 2); the α + βN model (R² {{fit:warm:medium:r2}}, β ≈ {{fit:warm:medium:betafrac}} of one hash to G2 plus one Miller loop, and the per-hop term dominating beyond N ≈ {{fit:warm:medium:cross}}); the constant 96-byte signature; inline certificates costing more per hop than aggregation saves ({{cert:bls}} against {{ablhop:medium}} bytes); a registry-free steady state; and the need for a non-aggregating baseline, which, measured, decides against aggregation. Arm E (Biscuit) ran faster than AIP's published figures, beyond the sanity rule's 3× in {{eflagged}}. The harness measures what D-68 specifies, but AIP's published "verify" is a larger operation: it decodes base64, verifies every block's signature twice, and parses its authorizer from Datalog text. That explains the direction of the gap and part of its size; the rest is unexplained (Q9).

## 2. Method

The method is the frozen plan's ([BENCH_PLAN_FROZEN.md](BENCH_PLAN_FROZEN.md), `bench-freeze-2`), with the post-measurement deviations in §7.
- **Grid.** {{configs}} latency configurations, over {{runs}} runs in separate processes, each in a seeded random order.
- **Counts.** 1,000 warm-up and 10,000 measured verifications per configuration, except Q5's injected-latency configurations (D-44).
- **Timing.** Every timed operation is one `verify` call on pre-generated bytes. Every measured verification was accepted, and every measured prefix-cache call hit or missed as its state requires.
- **Statistics.** Medians with bootstrap 95% CIs, pooled over runs. Each ratio gets a ±10% practical-significance margin, and its verdict stands only if all three runs agree (frozen plan §6).
- **Plots:** `results/plots/`.

**How the plan's open points were read:**
- **Break-even.** The first N in 1–10 at which A's and C's byte counts swap order.
- **Bytes.** Exact means over 20 chains (they vary only with identifier lengths), compared directly with no margin.
- **Claims.** §7's verdict criteria are stated beside each claim.
- **The two A-mt configurations flagged again** in their re-run use the re-run samples, like every re-run (marked †).
- **Run-to-run variation** is (max − min) of the run medians over the pooled median.

## 4. Answers to Q1–Q10

**Q1. Is aggregation faster per invocation, warm?** No, at every N and in every profile.
- **The deployment pattern.** With prefix caches, B costs {{lat:B:warm+prefix:3:medium}} µs against D's {{lat:D:warm+prefix:3:medium}} µs at N = 3, medium ({{vx:bd:3:medium}}×).
- **Every call a new chain.** A costs {{lat:A:warm:3:medium}} µs against C's {{lat:C:warm:3:medium}} µs ({{vx:ac:3:medium}}×) and C-batch's {{lat:C-batch:warm:3:medium}} µs ({{vx:acb:3:medium}}×).
- **Prefix misses.** B's are slower still: {{lat:B:prefix-miss:3:medium}} µs, because a miss runs the full path and then computes the prefix's Miller-loop product.
- **A-mt** (supplementary; `blst`'s thread pool) takes {{lat:A-mt:warm:3:medium}} µs at N = 3: faster than A, and still {{vx:amtc:3:medium}}× C.
- **Approval.** With an approval receipt (medium-approval, N = 3), every BLS arm pays one more single verification: B costs {{lat:B:warm+prefix:3:medium-approval}} µs, D {{lat:D:warm+prefix:3:medium-approval}} µs.

**Q2. How many bytes does aggregation save?** Against Ed25519 (C): nothing at N = 1, and a little from then on.
- **N = 1:** A is larger ({{bdiff:1:small}} bytes small, {{bdiff:1:medium-approval}} medium-approval).
- **Break-even N:** small {{breakeven:small}}, medium {{breakeven:medium}}, medium-approval {{breakeven:medium-approval}}, large {{breakeven:large}}.
- **At N = 10:** A is {{bsave:10:small}}% smaller (small), {{bsave:10:medium}}% (medium) and {{bsave:10:large}}% (large).
- **The signature's share:** {{share:A:3:medium}}% of A's chain at N = 3, medium, against {{share:C:3:medium}}% for C's N + 1 signatures. Policies dominate the bytes.
- **Against A-ind:** aggregation saves {{ablhop:medium}} bytes per hop.

**Q3. Does the α + βN model hold for arm A?** Yes.
- **Warm, medium:** α = {{fit:warm:medium:alpha}} µs {{fit:warm:medium:alphaci}}, β = {{fit:warm:medium:beta}} µs {{fit:warm:medium:betaci}}, R² {{fit:warm:medium:r2}}.
- **Which term dominates.** 10β/α = {{fit:warm:medium:tba}} {{fit:warm:medium:tbaci}}, so the per-hop term dominates by N = 10. The crossover is at N = α/β ≈ {{fit:warm:medium:cross}}.
- **Small and large:** β = {{fit:warm:small:beta}} and {{fit:warm:large:beta}} µs, with R² {{fit:warm:small:r2}} and {{fit:warm:large:r2}}.
- **Cold:** β = {{fit:cold:medium:beta}} µs, because each hop also pays its certificate's pairing check (P-28).
- **What β is made of.** It is {{fit:warm:small:betafrac}}–{{fit:warm:large:betafrac}} of one hash to G2 plus one Miller loop ({{hashml}} µs, from the micro-benchmarks: hash-to-G2 proxy {{crit:bls/hash_to_g2_proxy}} µs, Miller loop {{crit:bls/miller_loop}} µs). That fits a multi-Miller loop sharing work across pairs.

**Q4. What does the multi-pairing save over individual BLS?** It is a net benefit at every N, warm and cold. A/A-ind is {{v3range:aai:n2plus}} warm for N ≥ 2 and {{v3range:aaic}} cold, so the saving grows with N.

**Q5. How does the cold path behave with registry latency?** At N = 3, a cold verification makes {{calls:A:0:resolver}} resolver calls and {{calls:A:0:store}} policy-store call.
- **No injected latency:** A costs {{lat:A:cold+rtt0ms:3:medium}} µs and C {{lat:C:cold+rtt0ms:3:medium}} µs.
- **1 ms per call:** {{lat:A:cold+rtt1ms:3:medium}} against {{lat:C:cold+rtt1ms:3:medium}} µs.
- **80 ms per call:** {{lat:A:cold+rtt80ms:3:medium}} against {{lat:C:cold+rtt80ms:3:medium}} µs.

The sleeps dominate. Verification is local only in the steady state, as paper §8.2 says, and that steady state is what Q1 measures.

**Q6. Throughput?** At 10 threads (medium, N = 3), D verifies {{tp:D:10}} chains/s against B's {{tp:B:10}} ({{tprat:D:B:10}}×), and C {{tp:C:10}} against A's {{tp:A:10}} ({{tprat:C:A:10}}×). Adding the efficiency cores (14 threads) raises A's p99 per call from {{tpp99:A:10}} to {{tpp99:A:14}} µs, and D's from {{tpp99:D:10}} to {{tpp99:D:14}} µs.

**Q7. What does signing cost?**
- **Per hop**, including the digest: BLS {{crit:signing/bls/hop}} µs, Ed25519 {{crit:signing/ed25519/hop}} µs.
- **Adding to the BLS aggregate:** {{crit:signing/bls/aggregate_add}} µs.
- **An approval receipt:** {{crit:signing/bls/receipt}} µs (BLS), {{crit:signing/ed25519/receipt}} µs (Ed25519).
- **Issuance:** {{crit:signing/bls/issue}} µs, {{crit:signing/ed25519/issue}} µs.

**Q8. How do policy costs grow?**
- **Evaluate** stays in microseconds: {{crit:policy/evaluate/worst/r64_a8}} µs in the worst case at 64 rules × 8 atoms.
- **Contains** grows roughly with rules²: {{crit:policy/contains/worst/r4_a4}} µs at 4 × 4, {{crit:policy/contains/worst/r16_a4}} µs at 16 × 4, and {{crit:policy/contains/worst/r64_a8}} µs at 64 × 8.
- **Typical against worst.** For Contains they barely differ ({{crit:policy/contains/typical/r64_a8}} µs typical at 64 × 8).
- **In the benchmark,** the large profile's containment is part of every cold and miss path. Warm hits skip it.

**Q9. Arm E on this hardware against AIP's published figures.** The sanity rule (frozen plan §8) is triggered.
- **Where the gap lies.** E takes {{eaiprange:small:1,2,3,5}}× of AIP's time in the small profile, {{eaiprange:medium:1,2,3,5}}× in the medium profile and {{eaiprange:large:1,2,3,5}}× in the large profile. It is more than 3× faster in {{eflagged}}.
- **AIP's figures, checked.** SPEC §13.10's figures match arXiv:2603.24775v1, Table 5 ("Rust verify", "Size (Rust)"). AIP's evaluation text says only that chained mode ran 100 iterations per depth, on an Apple M3 Max under macOS 15.3, with biscuit-auth 6.0. What the timed "verify" contains is in AIP's code (github.com/sunilp/aip at `ad2faa6`, the commit that prepared the arXiv submission): `rust/aip-token/src/bin/bench_chained.rs` and `src/chained.rs`. - **Token sizes: a correction.** M10's version of this report said arm E's token sizes matched AIP's. The two are in different units: AIP's published sizes are base64 string lengths (`to_base64().len()`), and arm E's are raw bytes. In Biscuit's base64, arm E's small-profile token is {{esize64:1:small}} characters at depth 0 against AIP's {{aipsize:0}}, and {{esize64:5:small}} against {{aipsize:4}} at depth 4. Arm E's tokens are the larger ones, and they still verify faster.
- **What was checked in this harness**, following D-68:
  - **The mapping.** `crates/dc-baselines/tests/biscuit.rs` shows that arm E's tokens attenuate and agree with DC's Evaluate on the §6.2 policy family. Every measured verification was accepted.
  - **The timed scope.** The harness times `BiscuitArm::verify`: `Biscuit::from` (which deserializes the token and checks every block's signature with `verify_strict`), authorizer construction and `authorize`. The policies are parsed once, outside the timer, as D-68 says.
  - **The build.** The same release build as every arm, with `target-cpu=native`, biscuit-auth {{env:crates.biscuit-auth}} with its default features minus `pem`.
- **What the numbers show.** Each extra block costs E {{estep:small}} µs (small), about one Ed25519 `verify_strict` ({{crit:ed25519/verify_strict}} µs) plus Datalog evaluation. AIP's published per-block cost is {{aipstep}} µs, {{estepgap:small:0}}× E's. With a second `verify_strict` per block, as AIP's timed call makes, E's step would be {{estep2:small}} µs, and AIP's is still {{estepgap:small:1}}× that.
- **Conclusion.** The harness measures what D-68 specifies. No bug was found, and nothing was changed.
  - **The rule's premise does not hold.** The 3× rule assumes that the two figures time the same operation, and they do not. AIP's timed call does about twice arm E's decoding and signature work per block, plus fixed work that arm E's excludes: base64, a second root-key decode, Datalog parsing, and printing block 0.
  - **What that explains.** The direction of the gap, and part of its size.
  - **What it leaves unexplained.** The rest. AIP's timings are a mean of 100 single calls, each after creating a fresh token, on an older chip, and that could account for more; but none of it was measured in the frozen plan. AIP's own benchmark, run unmodified on this machine, is in §8 (exploratory).
- **Caveats.** The mapping was not adjusted. The hardware differs (an M4 Max here, an M3 Max in AIP). Arm E remains a positioning reference, with the functional gaps of paper Table 1.

## 6. Threats to validity

- **Hardware.** A single Apple M4 Max laptop.
  - There is no frequency governor and no core pinning on macOS; measuring threads ran at QoS user-interactive.
  - `target-cpu=native` reaches the Rust arms but not `blst`'s C and assembly (SPEC §3.3), so the build flags help the Ed25519 arms more than the BLS ones.
  - `blst` uses its armv8 assembly here, not the ADX path some x86 results rely on. curve25519-dalek uses its serial backend, because its SIMD backends are x86-only.
  - The ratios could differ on x86.
- **Laptop thermals.** Throttling was watched by pmset, which recorded no warning anywhere, and by a calibration probe (§3, "Throttling and interference"). Flagged configurations were re-run.
- **A-mt and heat.** A-mt's all-core load warms the chip, so A-mt's own latencies include that thermal state. The figures:

{{section:Threats to validity, from the harness's own readings}}

- **The registry is in-process.** Resolution and policy loading are function calls. Q5 injects latency by sleeping at least the stated delay per call, not by networking.
- **Synthetic policies.** The medium profile is the paper's §6.2 example; small and large are synthetic (D-71).
- **Arm E is not full AIP.** It is a Biscuit mapping, without registry resolution, PoP, revocation, receipts, the nonce cache or parameter binding. It ran faster than AIP's published figures, beyond the sanity rule's 3× in the small profile, and AIP's figures time a larger operation than arm E's (Q9).
- **The CIs are narrow because of pooling.** They come from a bootstrap of the pooled samples, which treats the three runs' samples as independent, and `mach_absolute_time` ticks at this machine's 24 MHz timebase (`hw.tbfrequency`), every 41.7 ns. So they are often degenerately narrow, and they understate between-run variation. The run-agreement rule guards the verdicts against this: every verdict's three run ratios are listed, and runs differed by at most a few percent (§3).
- **C-batch's semantics** differ from C's at the edges (D-66).
- **Cold rows include P-28's certificate pairings,** by design.
- **Runs 1 and 2 ran before the probe warm-up spin** (§7). Their Q5 configurations were flagged and re-run with it.
